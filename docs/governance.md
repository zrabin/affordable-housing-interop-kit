# Governance

## Governance Goal

The kit should be credible as a public-interest technical standard.

## Maintainer Role

Maintainers should:

- protect the privacy posture
- review changes for vendor neutrality
- keep examples synthetic
- avoid legal or regulatory overclaims
- preserve clarity for both technical and policy readers

## Contribution Rules

Contributions should:

- improve interoperability
- include tests or examples when changing schemas
- avoid implementation-specific assumptions
- keep terminology plain
- distinguish normative requirements from examples

The public standard boundary excludes:

- real applicant data
- implementation-private endpoint details
- unreviewed endorsement claims
- discriminatory logic
- agent tools that bypass human review for sensitive actions

## Decision Principles

When in doubt:

1. Prefer tenant safety.
2. Prefer auditability.
3. Prefer vendor neutrality.
4. Prefer explicit consent.
5. Prefer synthetic examples.
6. Prefer humble public-sector language.

## Becoming a Co-Steward

The Standard was initiated by Harmony, and the intent is for it not to stay a single-organization effort. The path from interested party to co-steward:

1. **Supporter.** Your organization endorses the principles in [CHARTER.md](../CHARTER.md) and is listed in [SUPPORTERS.md](../SUPPORTERS.md). No obligations attach; see the charter.
2. **Contributor.** You participate in shaping the Standard: feedback, issues, discussions, schema proposals, or an implementation. Participation by email counts the same as participation on GitHub.
3. **Co-steward.** Organizations with sustained, constructive participation and a demonstrated commitment to the maintainer responsibilities above (privacy posture, vendor neutrality, synthetic examples, public-interest framing) can take on shared maintainer rights and a voice in decisions.

Co-stewards are added by agreement of the existing maintainers, in the open, with the reasoning recorded. Competing vendors are explicitly welcome on this path: vendor neutrality is a thing co-stewards protect together, not a claim any one company can make alone.

If the community of supporters grows to the point where a more formal structure would serve it better (a steering group, a neutral fiscal home, or a standards body relationship), that evolution is on the table and will be decided with co-stewards, not for them.
